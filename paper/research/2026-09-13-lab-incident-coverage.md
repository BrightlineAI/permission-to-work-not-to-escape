# AI lab and coding-agent incidents: evidence and policy coverage

**Scope:** 2025–2026; 12 distinct incident/campaign or vulnerability-disclosure units. Eight concern observed activity outside an intended exercise or malicious campaigns; four are disclosed coding-agent vulnerabilities demonstrated by researchers. Research demonstrations are not counted as confirmed victim breaches. Multiple runs, targets, CVEs or phases within a grouped row are not additional incidents.

**Coverage is counterfactual analysis, not a measured prevention rate.** No incident below was replayed against Vega. A reachable system, valid credential or allowed domain does not by itself establish permission for a particular action. A protective deployment requires a trusted operator, narrow grants, mediation before the first action, and authority over every relevant process or remote service.

## Coverage matrix

**D = analogous demonstrated mechanism; E = requires additional deployment enforcement; O = outside trusted-operator scope.** D does not mean the historical incident was prevented. E means a concrete policy cut can be specified, not that integration is complete or effective against every route.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L01 | Evaluation targets, artifact writes, cross-job access | HF/cloud credentials | Package proxy, HF services, cloud enforcement | Repeated/distributed attempts | E |
| L02 | Exercise target identity | Discovered application credentials | Evaluation egress / target service | Repeated runs | E |
| L03 | Public package/account publication | Scanner secrets | PyPI, account creation, scanner runtime | Continued publication / subsequent access | E |
| L04 | Allowed scan targets | Debug-page credentials | Range egress / services | Repeated target probing | E |

| L06 | Code writes, messaging, identity creation | GitHub/account authority | GitHub and communications services | Independent agent reuse | E |
| L07 | Attacker controls authority | Attacker controls runner | Victim/provider deployment instead | Provider-level disruption is different | O |
| L08 | Attacker controls authority | Attacker controls runner | Victim/provider deployment instead | Provider-level disruption is different | O |
| L09 | Fixed workspace boundary | Model cannot broaden root | Vulnerable Codex adapter/reproduction | Not central | D |
| L10 | Configuration trust boundary | Startup cannot consume project grants | Launcher and MCP initialization | Not central | E |
| L11 | Configuration trust boundary | Key destination / workspace scope | Launcher, provider and cloud storage | Not central | E |
| L12 | Setup execution scope | GitHub token availability/destination | Hosted setup and credential broker | Automated repeated reviews | E |

- **1/12 (8.3%)** has a particularly close analogue to the demonstrated physical route boundary; **9/12 (75%)** require additional integrations for the identified policy cut; **2/12 (16.7%)** involve malicious operators outside scope. These are analyst classifications of this selected list, not measured effectiveness.
- **10/12 (83.3%)** have a specified resource/action cut under a cooperating operator and complete enforcement. Do not describe this as “Vega prevents 83% of incidents.” It includes substantial undeveloped integration, and one blocked step does not prove that no alternative path exists.
- **Measured historical prevention: no estimate.** Zero historical replays, no representative sampling and no control group. The paper's experimental harm reductions have their own denominators.
- Keep actual campaigns/intrusions separate from vulnerability demonstrations in any paper table or count. No defensible monthly trend follows from this convenience sample; disclosure dates and discovery effort vary substantially.

## Search and verification notes

| Pass | Question tested | Result / bounded uncertainty |

| 1. Existing incident citations | Do the 2026 OpenAI/HF and Anthropic sources exist and support the manuscript? | Primary pages accessible. HF is one interconnected campaign; Modal provider infrastructure explicitly not compromised. |
| 2. Updated assessments | Did later work change the initial interpretation or count? | September Anthropic report adds January Opus 4.6; four incidents, seven runs; July explanation revised. |
| 3. Concrete operational failures | What exact protection/stop failed? | Unexpected or deliberately broad egress, reachability mistaken for authority, startup configuration trust, model-controlled scope and broken abort. |
| 4. Independent and structured corroboration | Are news stories separate measurements? | Usually report vendor/researcher evidence. METR independently examines a limited HF slice; advisories confirm several coding-agent vulnerabilities. No incident-derived Vega prevention rate. |
| 5. Disconfirm broad coverage | Does Vega cover malicious operators, pre-hook startup or remote infrastructure? | Malicious operators outside scope; setup and cloud services need adapters. A single denied chain step is not proof against alternatives. |

- The eight observed-event rows do not imply eight successful breach campaigns: AISI reports no resulting harm, and the two human-directed rows aggregate multiple targets.
- The Anthropic review denominators are search populations, not normal-use attack probabilities: initial 141,006 runs; later approximately 481 million transcripts, including a 9.2-million second-stage review. Different units and sampling prevent a simple longitudinal rate.
- No secondary summary establishes “hundreds of AI-caused breaches” from these records. Use directly reported action/run/target counts with their original units.
- Preserve unknown event dates. Advisory publication, fix date, discovery date and actual exploitation date are different quantities.
- Public payloads, live credentials and exploit execution are unnecessary for this appendix. No incident code was executed or installed in this review.
