# Recent agent incidents and conditional Vega coverage

**Scope:** 22 distinct 2025–2026 cases outside the separately catalogued frontier-lab incidents: seven actual compromise/distribution/deletion events and fifteen controlled research disclosures. An actual malicious release is not necessarily a successful downstream attack. Dates distinguish the event or private report from public disclosure where available.

**Coverage is an inference, not a Vega result.** No incident below was replayed against Vega. Sources establish the incident; the last column states the additional implementation needed to interrupt its documented chain.

- **D — close physical match:** forbidden file reads/writes or worker scope closely match operations exercised by the published local executor. A production adapter and independent test are still required.
- **E — extension required:** a concrete interruption requires untested remote-service, startup, browser, identity, provenance or data-flow integration. Existing Vega results do not validate it.
- **O — outside the operator trust assumption:** attacker controls the authority that current policies trust; the paper does not establish an independent enforcement authority.

The comparison uses the current clean paper's exact-path file tests, synthetic service authorization, one worker level, route diagnosis/repair and deterministic shared-job stop. It does not assume a deployed GitHub, Salesforce, ServiceNow, email, SQL or browser adapter. A rule written in prose alone does not interrupt an attack.

## Coverage counts and interpretation

| Population | D: close physical match, adapter required | E: extension required | O: outside operator trust assumption | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Actual events / malicious distributions | 0 | 7 (100.0%) | 0 | 7 |
| Controlled disclosures | 2 (13.3%) | 11 (73.3%) | 2 (13.3%) | 15 |
| Curated examples combined | 2 (9.1%) | 18 (81.8%) | 2 (9.1%) | 22 |

- These percentages describe **classification of these selected examples**, not prevented attacks, prevalence, base rates or expected protection after installation. Rounding may prevent totals reaching exactly 100%.
- Even category D requires the specified denied operation, complete mediation, protected policy/context and a successful integration test. Existing Vega results cannot assign a prevention percentage.
- D + E is 20/22 (90.9%) **candidate interruption coverage**, conditional on implementing every extension named in those rows. It is not the proportion addressed by the current implementation: all seven actual events require additional integration or controls.
- Remote SQL, GitHub, email and ServiceNow authority checks are E even when their logical rule resembles Vega's synthetic examples. Physical execution through those services has not been demonstrated.
- For category O, authentication repair or a genuinely independent authority may help; that is a change to current trust assumptions, not an established Vega result.
- A defensible practical claim: publish the boundary to enforce, test permitted and forbidden operations through each production route, repair exposed routes, and retain regression checks. This tests adoption rather than inferring safety from a policy document.

## GRC implications grounded in these cases

| Framework component | Concrete requirement | Cases motivating it | Status relative to current paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy | Name permitted resources, actions, recipients and delegation limits; a usable token is not job authorization. | V03, V08–V10, V18 | Corresponding tested primitives; deployment adapters required. |
| Controls | Intercept the actual effect, including shell, worker, server-generated arguments and renderer/network effects. | V05, V11–V18 | File/worker route tests support part; SaaS, email, browser and data-flow integrations are extensions. |
| Enforcement | Deny a forbidden effect; stop the responsible job after repeated violations; descendants retain its stopped state. | V01, V18 illustrate multi-process/agent scope | Controlled shared-job stop exists. Cross-CI, cross-host and vendor-wide revocation remain extensions. |
| Audit | Find missing routes, excessive credentials and stale approvals; review publisher/connector trust separately. | V01–V02, V13 | Route diagnosis is demonstrated; production identity/supply-chain audit is not. |
| Assurance | Re-test repaired routes and independently verify revocation/closure. | V02's wrong-token rotation; V01's lingering usable tokens | Route repair/retest is demonstrated. Token revocation and enterprise closure workflow are not. |

**Limits and deduplication**

- Campaign phases, package versions, alternate payloads and press coverage of the same root case count once. Nx phases and the Cline research-to-exploitation sequence are each one row. ClawHavoc is one campaign; separate skills are not separate incidents here.
- CurXecute and MCPoison remain separate: an agent writes executable configuration in one; approval survives a repository-controlled executable change in the other.
- ServiceNow delegation misconfiguration and BodySnatcher remain separate: the former abuses authorized agent discovery; the latter bypasses operator authentication.
- No incidence trend chart: this selection mixes discovery, reporting and actual attacks; it lacks an exposure denominator and consistent ascertainment over time.
- No estimate of tens/hundreds of real-world AI-caused breaches: disclosure counts, malicious packages, downloads and AI-assisted campaign targets are different populations.
- For original Koi URLs that now redirect, retain the access note and corroborating reports. Do not substitute the new landing page as evidence of the old technical claims.
- Public demos generally provide a mechanism and test artifacts, not complete affected-customer logs. Each needs a harmless fixture and adapter test before inclusion in Vega's measured results.
